# Further reading

Pointers, not method. The modules in `core/` are self-contained; nothing here needs to be
consulted to use them. This file exists so the bundle's sources are attributable and so an
author who wants to go deeper knows where.

## Sources this bundle draws on

- **Larry McEnerney, University of Chicago Writing Program** — reader-value framework:
  function and value, constructing the problem, stasis versus background, the danger of gap.
  Underlies `core/engineer-argument/`, especially `core/engineer-argument/references/principles.md`. His lecture
  "The Craft of Writing Effectively" is the widely circulated short version.
- **Joseph M. Williams, *Style: Toward Clarity and Grace*** — characters as subjects, old
  before new, the stress position, topic strings, nominalization. Underlies
  `core/polish-prose/`, especially `core/polish-prose/references/sentence-craft.md`.
- **Daryl J. Bem, "Writing the Empirical Journal Article"** (in Darley, Zanna & Roediger,
  *The Compleat Academic*, 2nd ed., APA, 2003) — the hourglass shape, opening statements,
  audience level, rewriting. Underlies parts of
  `core/develop-manuscript/references/section-functions.md`.
  **Read its opening section with care:** its advice to write "the article that makes the most
  sense now that you have seen the results" predates the replication crisis and, taken
  literally, licenses HARKing. See
  `core/develop-manuscript/references/provenance-rules.md` § *Exploration and the confirmatory
  record* for what to keep and what to reject.
- **Harvard Department of Psychology, *Writing for Psychology: A Guide for Psychology
  Concentrators*** (Carson, Fama, Clancy, Ebert & Tierney, 2012) — critical source reading.
  Underlies `core/audit-manuscript/references/source-quality.md`. The rest of the guide is
  aimed at undergraduate coursework (library search, academic honesty, APA basics) and is out
  of scope here.

## General craft

Recommended in the *Canadian Urological Association Journal* Writing Resource Kit, which is
itself a reading list rather than a method.

- **William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*** — the first ten chapters are the useful part.
- **Strunk & White, *The Elements of Style*** — source of "omit needless words." Prescriptive
  in places, and contemporary style writers push back on it; still the shortest route to
  concision.
- **Steven Pinker, *The Sense of Style*** — the modern counterweight to Strunk & White,
  including a fair treatment of when the passive is correct.
- **Helen Sword, *Stylish Academic Writing*** and **The Writer's Diet** — strong verbs,
  concrete nouns, restraint with modifiers and waste words. The Writer's Diet test operates on
  roughly the same signals as `scripts/textmetrics.py --focus sentences`; if you like one you
  will find the other familiar.
- **Anne E. Greene, *Writing Science in Plain English*** — under 100 pages, exercises
  throughout, closely aligned with `core/polish-prose/`.
- **Paul J. Silvia, *Write It Up*** and ***How to Write a Lot*** — structuring for impact, and
  the habit side of production.
- **Barbara W. Sarnecka, *The Writing Workshop*** — wide-ranging, with per-genre specifics.

## On the act of writing

- **Anne Lamott, *Bird by Bird***, and **Stephen King, *On Writing*** — not academic, and not
  about argument. Useful when the obstacle is starting rather than structuring.

## A note on using these

None of these is a citable source for a manuscript's own argument. They are revision methods.
`core/engineer-argument/SKILL.md` makes the same point about McEnerney specifically: attribute
the framework once if it comes up, then just use it.
